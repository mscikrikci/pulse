# AI-Assisted Training Programming: A Case Study
**How an AI coaching system was built through conversation, feedback loops, and progressive refinement over 3+ weeks**

---

## Overview

This document is a template and case study showing how AI-assisted training programming works in practice. It covers the full arc from initial intake through weekly program design, session-by-session feedback, real-time adjustments, and the reasoning behind every major decision.

The format is conversational — showing what the user brought to each interaction, how the AI analyzed it, what decisions were made, what was challenged, and what changed as a result.

---

## Part 1: Initial Intake and Goal Setting

### Trainee Profile

**User provided:**
- Male, 46 years old, 183cm, ~100kg (later confirmed 106kg at ~30% body fat)
- 10+ years CrossFit background, 5-7 years combat training
- Training break of unspecified duration before reentry
- Access to two gym facilities (office and residential) with varying equipment
- Swimming background — 800m in 17 min, 2400m in 50 min

**Equipment confirmed through conversation:**
- Barbells with plates (up to 120-140kg available)
- Dumbbells 10-25kg
- 24kg kettlebell
- 9kg wall ball
- Pull-up bar with resistance bands
- Stationary bike
- Dual cable machine with independent handles
- Dips station
- No squat rack — power clean required for front squat loading
- 15m pool (later deprioritized)

### Goal Evolution — A Key Early Shift

**User initially stated:** Open water swimming goal (Bosphorus 3.4km), longevity, functional strength.

**AI provided a pre-built 4-week program** focused on swimming technique and strength support.

**User then clarified:** Swimming is not the goal for the next few months. Long-term goal is to stay active for life — protecting existing muscle and strength, not necessarily improving.

**AI response:** Acknowledged the significant goal shift. Reframed the programming priority order:
1. Muscle mass preservation and bone density — the primary longevity levers at 46
2. Functional strength — movements that carry over to real life
3. Cardiovascular health — through walking, rucking, and metcon rather than sport-specific training

**AI flagged a blindspot:** "Protecting existing muscle" is actually harder to program than improvement because the minimum effective dose is less obvious. Too little and you lose tissue. Too much and injury risk climbs. The margin narrows with age.

## Part 2: Reentry Week — Days 1-6

### Philosophy Established Before Day 1

The AI established several non-negotiable principles before any session was designed:

**Pavel Tsatsouline submaximal strength principles** applied to all barbell work:
- Never train to failure on strength movements
- Stop when reps feel like 7/10 effort
- If a rep slows significantly, the set is over
- Long rest periods (3 min) between heavy sets
- Frequent, submaximal practice builds neural efficiency without systemic fatigue

**User challenged this directly**, citing a podcast episode where Pavel explained his low-rep, long-rest approach for building maximal strength. The AI engaged with the challenge rather than deferring:

> "Where you're right: submaximal work is exactly the right approach for strength and neural adaptation. Where the controversy lives: Pavel's approach is optimized for strength — neural efficiency and skill acquisition. Your stated goal includes hypertrophy. The research is clear that proximity to failure on accessory movements is necessary for hypertrophic stimulus. The honest synthesis: Pavel principles for strength movements (deadlift, squat, press), closer to failure for hypertrophy accessories."

This became the foundational programming structure going forward.

### Day 1 — Full Body Complex (Self-directed, Pre-program)

**User performed independently:** DB bench + DB row, DB shoulder press, KB front rack, Romanian deadlift, rotational cable movement.

**AI analysis:** No HR data for this session. Used it as a baseline signal — the combination of movements informed which muscle groups needed recovery time before programming the next session.

**Key decision:** Day 2 was programmed as metcon rather than strength, deliberately avoiding the pressing tissue loaded on Day 1.

### Day 2 — Metcon

**Program designed:**
- Every 2 minutes for 10 rounds: 15 KB swings + 30 double unders + 10 dead bugs

**User pushed back on dead bugs:** "I never feel anything with dead bug."

**AI response:** Swapped immediately to V-ups. Noted that dead bug's low perceived effort is a legitimate reason to drop it — training feedback from the athlete is a valid input, not just preference.

**User added:** Planned accessories after (dead hang, neck bridge, wall sit) plus cycling buy-out.

**AI flagged neck bridge concern** given accumulated fatigue. User clarified they perform supported neck bridge with significant load through hands — AI acknowledged this as a safe and smart variation and kept it in the program.

**Session outcome:**
- Scaled double unders to 20 (degraded quickly under fatigue)
- Stopped at round 8
- Started with 20 KB swings, scaled to 15 for time management
- V-ups were challenging due to hip flexor stiffness, not core strength

**HR data (Apple Watch):**
- HIIT block: 148 BPM avg, Zone 3-4 dominant, peaked at 167
- Cycling buy-out: 145 BPM avg, almost entirely Zone 3
- Post-workout recovery: 149 BPM at minute zero, 135 at minute 2 — slow recovery curve

**AI interpretation:** The cycling was supposed to be Zone 2 but came in at Zone 3. The AI initially flagged this as a concern. User corrected: between metcon and cycling they performed the isometrics block, and their personal Zone 2 on the bike sits at approximately 140-145 BPM based on nasal breathing comfort — higher than the generic Apple Watch Zone 2 range. AI accepted the correction and reframed: subjective nasal breathing comfort is a more accurate Zone 2 marker than population-average HR numbers.

**Key learning documented:** User's Zone 2 sits higher than textbook ranges. Use nasal breathing sustainability as the true marker going forward, not HR thresholds.

### Day 3 — Rest

User reported: phone trembling when holding it after the day's accumulated fatigue. AI flagged this as significant — not normal post-session fatigue but a sign that shoulder stabilizers and forearm flexors were genuinely depleted across multiple consecutive days of grip and shoulder loading.

**Pattern identified:** Shoulders and grip are the primary weekly fatigue limiters. This became a persistent programming constraint.

### Day 4 — Loaded Walk (Rucking)

**User plan:** 5km, 60 min, 6-8kg single hand carry, switching hands.

**AI refinement:** Suggested longer unbroken carry sets (400-500m per hand) rather than frequent switching, to build more lateral stability endurance. Also introduced the bent-arm carry variation — user revealed they were intentionally keeping knees slightly bent to load biceps during the carry.

**AI corrected an error:** Initially framed the concern as "at 100kg bodyweight, sustained elbow flexor tension..." User correctly challenged the bodyweight reference — bodyweight is irrelevant to elbow flexor loading during a carry. The relevant variables are load duration and carry weight. AI acknowledged the error directly.

**Session outcome:**
- 5.14km, 57 min, 107m elevation, 124 BPM avg
- Zone 1 dominant, Zone 2 briefly at the climb
- Shoulders felt fatigue first, grip followed after ~15 min, shoulders then adapted
- Nasal breathing broke on the steep climb — mouth breathing started

**HR data:** Post-workout HR still 135 BPM at 2 minutes — consistent with accumulated week fatigue.

### Day 5 — Lower Body Strength (Moved from Day 5 to Day 6 due to carry fatigue)

**User reported Day 4 fatigue:** Grip, shoulders, bicep soreness. AI recommended pushing Day 5 by one day.

**Program designed:**

**Strength Block — Pavel Principles:**

| Set | Load | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| Deadlift 1 | 60kg | 5 |
| Deadlift 2 | 80kg | 4 |
| Deadlift 3 | 100kg | 3 |
| Deadlift 4 | 110-120kg | 2-3 |

**No rack available — solution:**

User asked about back squat. AI noted no rack available. User suggested Smith machine. AI explained Smith machine trade-offs (reduced stabilizer demand, acceptable for hypertrophy). User then clarified neither Smith machine nor leg press available on this day — free weights only.

**AI proposed front squat via power clean.** User confirmed power clean is their solution for getting bar into position.

**Key discussion — strength and hypertrophy in the same session:**

User challenged whether combining Pavel submaximal strength work with hypertrophy accessories in the same session actually makes sense.

**AI response:** The traditional argument for separating them is sound in theory — different rep ranges, different rest periods, different fatigue profiles. But the research is less clean than the theory. What matters more: heavy compound work creates a potentiation effect, and the question is whether you have enough left after deadlifts and front squats to take split squats close to failure. For this specific session, deadlift and front squat are different movement patterns from split squats and lunges — the overlap is real but not total. Sequencing holds: heavy barbell first while fresh, unilateral hypertrophy after. The session was kept as designed.

**Hypertrophy Block:**

| Movement | Sets x Reps | Load |
|---|---|---|
| Bulgarian Split Squat | 3 x 10/side | 2 x 20kg DB |
| Reverse Lunge | 3 x 10/side | 2 x 15kg DB |

**Session outcome:**
- Deadlift reached 105kg (weight availability limited, not capacity)
- BSS: had to drop weight entirely and do bodyweight — balance was the issue, not strength
- Lunges at 15kg: OK but tiring after BSS
- Left leg significantly weaker than right on both movements — noted as key finding

**HR data:**
- 55 min, 105 BPM avg, Zone 1 dominant, peaked at 154
- Post-workout HR dropped to 73 BPM almost immediately — excellent recovery response, much better than Day 2 metcon

**Key finding documented:** Left/right lower body strength asymmetry is significant. Unilateral work will be a consistent programming priority with extra left side volume.

### Day 6 — Rest + Light Walk

User chose this independently. AI endorsed it. Short walk stats: 2.43km, 41 min, 97 BPM avg, pure Zone 1, rated Easy. Post-workout HR dropped to 84 BPM within 2 minutes — excellent signal that the week's accumulated load had been absorbed.

### Reentry Week — Summary Assessment

**What the reentry week revealed:**
- Shoulders and grip are the primary weekly fatigue limiters — appear in every session
- Left/right lower body asymmetry is meaningful — needs consistent unilateral corrective work
- Cardiovascular system is in better shape than muscular endurance
- Recovery capacity is returning — later days showed faster HR recovery than early days
- DOMS duration is individually long (historically 8-9 days) — programming needs to account for this
- Zone 2 HR sits higher than generic ranges — nasal breathing is the true marker

## Part 3: Program Design Principles — How the AI Built the Structure

### Weekly Architecture

After reentry, the AI proposed a 3-4 day strength structure. User confirmed 3 days realistic, 4 doable.

**Settled structure:**
- 3 strength days (upper/lower split or full body)
- Rucking/walking as the cardio and lower body volume driver — unlimited, no weekly cap
- Metcon 1x per week for conditioning
- Rest days used for active recovery, accessories, and stretching

**Why this works for longevity at 46:**
- 3 days of meaningful strength work is the optimal frequency for recovery at this age without pharmaceutical support
- More than 4 days creates recovery debt that accumulates faster than adaptation
- Walking and rucking preserve bone density and posterior chain without joint stress — treated as a cornerstone, not an afterthought

### Equipment Ceiling and Its Implications

**Upper body:** 25kg DB ceiling limits hypertrophy stimulus on larger muscle groups. However, cable machine with independent handles provides constant tension alternative — actually superior to DBs for some movements.

**Lower body:** 25kg DBs severely limit leg hypertrophy. Barbell deadlift and front squat provide the primary stimulus. BSS and lunges are the hypertrophy tools — grip becomes the limiting factor before legs on these.

**AI flag:** Leg press or Smith machine would solve the lower body hypertrophy ceiling problem. User confirmed Smith machine may be available — to be confirmed.

### Rotational Strength — A Programming Problem Solved Through Dialogue

The AI initially programmed rotational strength (cable woodchop or KB swing across body) as a standalone finisher at the end of strength sessions.

**Problem:** It was the first thing dropped when sessions ran long. Happened twice in week 2 and week 3.

**User insight:** "Rotational strength is more strength work — maybe I can go with moderate weight and long duration, supersetting with front squat. I have cable available in both gyms."

**AI response:** Immediately recognized this as better programming logic. Front squat is pure sagittal plane. Cable woodchop is pure transverse plane. Zero muscle group overlap — one recovers while the other works. Superset format ensures rotational work is embedded in the session rather than appended to it.

**Further refinement — alternating directions:**
- Low to high (cable at ankle height): hip and glute drive, lower oblique dominant — Tuesdays
- High to low (cable at shoulder height): lat and upper oblique drive — Wednesdays

Both directions of rotation trained every week. One-directional rotational training creates imbalances over time.

### Heart Rate Data as a Programming Input

Every session with HR data was analyzed across four dimensions:

1. **Zone distribution** — how much time in each zone tells you the energy system being trained
2. **Peak HR** — identifies which movement drove the highest demand
3. **Post-workout recovery curve** — 2-minute post-workout HR drop is a proxy for cardiovascular fitness and session fatigue level
4. **Cardiac drift** — HR rising over time at the same effort level indicates accumulated fatigue or dehydration

**Key HR findings used in programming:**
- Day 2 metcon post-workout HR still 135 at 2 min → session was harder than planned, scale back next week
- Day 5 lower body post-workout HR dropped to 73 immediately → Pavel principles working, systemic fatigue well managed
- Week 2 upper body post-workout HR dropped to 100 in 2 min → strength session appropriately submaximal
- Week 2 metcon Zone 4 dominant → man makers more demanding than KB swings, manage lat volume

### Week 2 Key Discoveries

**Discovery 1 — Bench press pectoral activation issue:**

User reported after Tuesday's session: "I suspect I cannot use my pectoral muscles as much as I can use shoulders — feel like shoulders do more than they should."

AI diagnosis: Classic shoulder-dominant bench pattern, usually caused by one of three things — grip width too narrow, failure to retract scapula before unracking, or bar path going straight up rather than following a slight arc.

Solution embedded in program: Scapula retraction cue before every set. "Bend the bar" cue during every rep. Practice at 60kg until chest sensation is clear before loading higher.

**Week 3 outcome:** User reported feeling chest tension during bench press for the first time. Reached 80kg cleanly — a weight that caused technique breakdown in week 2 session 1.

**Discovery 2 — Dual cable lat pulldown as primary pulling variation:**

User couldn't complete band-assisted pull-ups even with assistance. Pivoted to dual cable lat pulldown with independent handles.

AI reframed this: Not a compromise — superior choice for someone with a known left/right pulling asymmetry. Standard bar allows the stronger side to compensate silently. Independent cables force each side to work honestly. Named as the preferred pulling variation going forward.

**Discovery 3 — Man makers load lats and calves, not just chest:**

User reported day-after soreness in lats and calves after the Friday metcon, not chest or shoulders.

AI initially misattributed the lat soreness to Tuesday's pulling work (4 days prior). User correctly identified it as coming from Friday's man makers.

AI analysis: 4 reps x 8 rounds = 32 DB rows under metabolic fatigue — significant lat volume. Calves loaded through continuous postural stabilization holding 30kg in forward hinge position — center of mass shifts forward, calves and tibialis anterior work isometrically to prevent tipping. Programming implication: Tuesday barbell rows + Friday man makers = meaningful lat load twice per week. Monitor as volume or load increases.

**Discovery 4 — Cold shower and hypertrophy:**

User asked about cold shower impact after hypertrophy training.

AI response: Cold water immersion blunts the hypertrophic stimulus by suppressing the inflammatory response that is part of the muscle protein synthesis signal. A cold shower (vs full immersion) has a much weaker effect and is probably not meaningfully impacting hypertrophy signal. Practical rule: cool or lukewarm shower after strength/hypertrophy, cold shower freely after metcon or cardio.

### Week 3 Key Discoveries

**Discovery 1 — Dips harder than expected:**

User attempted dips for the first time — 3 reps, depth insufficient.

AI analysis: Two separate issues identified:
- Strength: 3 reps at 106kg is honest data. Bodyweight dips are a serious load.
- Mobility: Shoulder extension range of motion limits depth before strength does at this stage.

AI instruction: Do not force depth. Shallow dips with shoulder compensation is a rotator cuff risk. Build mobility first, reps second. 3 controlled reps to comfortable depth beats 8 shallow reps every time.

**Discovery 2 — Cable as a hypertrophy equivalent:**

DBs were occupied. User substituted cable incline press and cable seated row with independent handles.

AI reframed: Not a compromise — cables provide constant tension throughout the range of motion, unlike DBs where resistance drops off at the top. Added to program formally as "DB or cable equivalent" — both valid, neither preferred.

**Discovery 3 — Push-up endurance as a weak point:**

User expected to complete 20 push-ups in a fresh set. During the metcon (push-ups first in the round, no prior fatigue in that session) they struggled from round one.

AI analysis: This is a strength endurance deficit, not a strength deficit. 80kg bench press is respectable. But muscular endurance for repeated bodyweight reps across multiple rounds is a different quality — volume-dependent, drops faster than strength during detraining.

Solution proposed: Pavel's greasing the groove applied to push-ups — 3-5 sets of 5-7 push-ups spread throughout the day on non-pressing days. Never to failure. Frequent submaximal practice. Significant improvement expected within 4-6 weeks.

**Discovery 4 — Wall balls smooth for first time:**

User reported wall balls at 9kg felt almost smooth. AI noted: genuine progress signal. Three weeks ago rebuilding from scratch. Squat pattern, timing, and shoulder coordination now working together.

## Part 6: Ongoing Tracking and Signals

### HR Data Interpretation Framework

Every session's HR data was analyzed consistently. The framework:

| Metric | What It Tells You |
|---|---|
| Average HR | Overall intensity level of the session |
| Zone distribution | Which energy system was primarily trained |
| Peak HR | Which movement drove the highest cardiovascular demand |
| Post-workout HR at 0, 1, 2 min | Recovery capacity and session fatigue level |
| Cardiac drift | HR rising over time at same pace = accumulated fatigue or dehydration |

**Comparative benchmarks established:**
- Post-workout HR dropping to <100 BPM within 2 min = well-recovered session, load appropriate
- Post-workout HR still >130 BPM at 2 min = session was harder than intended or systemic fatigue high
- Zone 1 dominant strength session = Pavel principles working correctly
- Zone 3-4 dominant metcon = appropriate for conditioning sessions

### Weekly Fatigue Limiters — Tracked Across All Sessions

| Limiter | Sessions It Appears | Management Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder fatigue | Every session in some form | Monitor weekly accumulation, insert full rest day if tremors return |
| Grip fatigue | Deadlifts, carries, man makers, hang | Mixed grip for deadlift, alternate carry styles, monitor weekly volume |
| Left/right lower body asymmetry | BSS, lunges, single-leg RDL | Extra left side volume and extended hold duration every session |
| Lat fatigue | Tuesday rows + Friday man makers | Upper/lower day swap in week 3, monitor lat soreness into next session |
| Thoracic mobility restriction | Thread the needle | Daily practice, don't force range — let it improve gradually |
| Push-up endurance | Metcon pressing rounds | Greasing the groove protocol on rest days |

## Part 7: Programming Principles — Consolidated Reference

### Strength Sessions
- Pavel submaximal: never grind, stop at 7/10 effort, 3 min rest between heavy sets
- Technique quality is the non-negotiable — load is secondary
- Warm-up sets are information-gathering, not just preparation
- How the first heavy set moves tells you what's available that day

### Hypertrophy Sessions
- Last 2 reps should be genuinely challenging
- 3 sec eccentric on DB movements
- Antagonist supersets (press/pull) for time efficiency without strength compromise
- DB and cable are interchangeable — choose based on availability
- Build depth and control before adding load (especially dips)

### Metcon Sessions
- Not a strength session — deliberately different primary muscle groups
- Scale within the session based on HR and form, not ego
- Stopping early is correct when form breaks — quality over completion
- Round order matters: do pressing when freshest within each round

### Loaded Walks
- Zone 2 marker: nasal breathing sustainability, not HR number
- Longer unbroken carry sets (400-500m) build more lateral stability than frequent switching
- Alternate bent-arm and straight-arm carry to distribute elbow flexor load
- Switch hands before form degrades, not after

### Recovery
- Active recovery (light walk, accessories) between sessions reduces DOMS faster than complete rest
- Post-session stretching is mandatory, not optional — organized by muscle groups loaded
- Daily 5-minute mobility routine (hip 90/90, thread the needle, couch stretch) covers most longevity bases
- Left side always last in every stretch, always 30 extra seconds — asymmetry correction is ongoing

### Rotational Strength
- Embedded as superset with front squat (not a standalone finisher) — prevents it being dropped under time pressure
- Low to high (ankle height) on lower body days — hip and glute drive
- High to low (shoulder height) on upper body days — lat and oblique drive
- Both directions trained every week for balanced rotational development

## Part 9: What This Template Demonstrates

This case study shows how AI-assisted training programming differs from a generic program:

**1. It's built from real data, not assumptions.**
Every load, every set scheme, every scaling decision was informed by what actually happened in previous sessions — not by population averages or generic templates.

**2. The athlete is the primary expert on their own body.**
Multiple times the user corrected the AI's analysis. Those corrections were accepted and updated. The AI brought structure, principles, and cross-session pattern recognition. The user brought real-time sensation data, historical self-knowledge, and equipment constraints.

**3. Programs are living documents.**
The markdown files were updated after every session with actual loads, scaling decisions, HR data, and notes. Future sessions were programmed based on what the previous session revealed — not on what was planned.

**4. Pushback improves the program.**
The cable woodchop superset integration, the Zone 2 HR recalibration, the correction on lat soreness attribution — all of these came from the user challenging the AI's output. The document got better every time.

**5. Principles beat prescriptions.**
Rather than "do exactly this," the program embedded reasoning: why this movement, why this load, why this order, what to watch for, what to do if X happens. This allowed the athlete to make good in-session decisions independently without needing real-time coaching.
